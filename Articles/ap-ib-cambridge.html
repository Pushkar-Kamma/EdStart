AP (Advanced Placement)

  • What it is: College-level high-school courses. You choose 1 or many.
  • How classes/exams work: Take the class; exam is in May. Some schools allow exam-only by request.
  • Difficulty: Varies by subject. Expect faster pace and more independent reading/problem-solving.
  • Costs/fee waivers: Schools post fees each fall; Washington fully subsidizes AP/IB/Cambridge exam fees for eligible low-income students (ask early).
  • College credit: Many WA colleges grant credit or placement with qualifying scores (policy varies by campus/major).
  • Sign-up: Add AP courses during spring registration; confirm exam order/payment in the fall (see BSD/LWSD links below).

IB (International Baccalaureate)

  • What it is: A structured, two-year program with six subjects and a core (TOK, EE, CAS).
  • How it works: Assessments include external exams and internal work; most exams occur second year.
  • Who it fits: Students who enjoy seminar-style classes, writing, and global themes across subjects.
  • Local note: IB is housed at Interlake High School (BSD). Planning ahead for math and world language helps (see Interlake IB page).
  • Costs/fee waivers: Posted by the school; state subsidies apply for eligible students.
  • College credit: Granted by exam level/score; policies vary by campus.

Cambridge / AICE (AS & A Levels)

  • What it is: Cambridge InternationalGÇÖs advanced courses. Students combine AS (one-year) and A Levels (two-year).
  • AICE Diploma: Earned by passing across subject groups; includes Global Perspectives & Research.
  • Who it fits: Students who want depth and choice across disciplines with strong exam preparation.
  • Local note: The Cambridge Program runs at Juanita High School (LWSD). It uses an application/lottery and a published four-year pathway.
  • Costs/fee waivers: Posted by the school; state subsidies apply for eligible students.
  • College credit: Many US universitiesGÇöincluding WA publicsGÇöaward credit for AS/A Levels (policy varies).

Other Options to Compare

  • Honors: Faster pace and deeper content. Looks good on transcripts; usually no exam-based college credit.
  • CTE Dual Credit: Career/technical classes that may transcript to a partner community/technical college if you meet competency rules.
  • College in the High School (CiHS): College courses taught at your high school; you register with the partner college for credit.
  • Running Start (11GÇô12th): Take classes at a WA community/technical college; tuition covered up to state limits (books/fees usually not covered).

How to Choose

  1. Start with availability. If you attend Interlake, IB is built-in; at Juanita, Cambridge is built-in. Most other BSD/LWSD high schools offer AP.
  2. Pick for fit. Love writing and a cohesive program? IB. Want a custom mix or depth in a few areas? AP or Cambridge.
  3. Plan for credit. Open the credit charts for your target colleges/majors before you lock in exams.
  4. Balance your life. ItGÇÖs fine to start with 1GÇô2 advanced courses and scale up.
  5. Ask for support. Use fee waivers/subsidies and talk to counselors and coordinators early.
